## **Overview**

*This summary is here so you know what you are signing. It is not the agreement — Sections 1 through 13 are — and if the two ever seem to differ, the agreement controls.*

  - **You are in charge of your own work.** No one here supervises you. You set your own hours, methods, and place (Section 1).
  - **You are paid from the Collective Seat:** a share of the Company-wide split, earned at your Threshold for every period you work. What you earn never expires. Lean years do not erase it, and your Unpaid Balance keeps being paid after the engagement ends, and after you become a Member, until it reaches zero (Section 3).
  - **The Company owns what you make for it, music included**, so it can put your music in the Game and sell the soundtrack, including as a Kickstarter backer reward. Your own prior tools and materials stay yours (Section 5).
  - **If our Kickstarter fails, you become a co-owner of your music**, compositions and recordings. You can then sell, stream, and license it yourself and keep everything it earns, without asking anyone. The Company and its Members can still use it too. Your pay is the same either way (Section 6).
  - **Only our last campaign counts.** We may fail and relaunch. A campaign that ends unfunded, with no new campaign live within 30 days, is a failure (Section 6).
  - **You can always post about the Game, its music, and how they were made**, and use them in your portfolio — anywhere, any time, monetized or not, with no approval needed (Section 7).
  - **You get credit by name** on anything you worked on that ships (Section 8).
  - **You are on the path to ownership from day one.** You do not apply and nobody has to approve it: candidacy starts automatically when you start work, and the Company votes on admitting you before your sixth month is out. Equal seat, equal share, equal vote, no buy-in (Section 9).
  - **Nobody works here as a non-owner past six months.** If you are not admitted by then, the engagement ends — unless you told us in writing that you did not want membership. This Company does not keep a permanent contractor class (Sections 2.4 and 9).
  - **There is no payroll here.** The Company has no employees and pays no wages or salary to anyone, Members included. You are paid out of what the work earns (Section 3).
  - **You can always talk about your pay and your working conditions**, to anyone, including other workers. Confidentiality here covers unreleased work, credentials, and other people’s personal data, and never stops you posting about the Game (Sections 7 and 10).
  - **You can work for anyone else you like**, including other studios (Section 11).
  - **No one may punish you for raising a concern** (Section 13.6).

## **Background**

The Company is a worker-owned cooperative governed by its Operating Agreement (the “Operating Agreement”), whose mission is to generate more games, sustainably, with genuine depth. The Company engages contributors both for defined work and as prospective long-term partners: this Agreement includes a defined path toward candidacy for membership (Section 9), and nothing in it is intended to create a permanent class of non-owner labor.

## **1. Engagement and Services**

The Company engages the Contributor, and the Contributor agrees, to perform the services described in Exhibit 1 (the “Services”), including any deliverables and timeline stated there (the “Deliverables”). The Contributor determines the manner, means, schedule, and location of performance. The Contributor may consult the Members for direction, but no Member supervises the Contributor.

## **2. Term and Termination**

1.  This Agreement runs from the Start Date until the Services are complete or the Agreement is terminated.
2.  Either party may terminate this Agreement on fourteen (14) days’ written notice. The Company may instead terminate immediately for the Contributor’s unlawful conduct, or for a material breach the Contributor has not cured within ten (10) days after written notice describing it. The Company decides to terminate, for any reason, by Supermajority vote — the same way it decided to admit the Contributor.
3.  Sections 3 through 8, 10, 12, and 13 survive termination.
4.  **Six-month ceiling.** This Agreement terminates at the end of the sixth month after the Start Date unless the Contributor has been admitted as a Member (Section 9). The only exception is where the Contributor has declined membership in writing, in which case the Members may extend this Agreement by Supermajority in renewable terms of no more than six (6) months each. Termination under this paragraph ends the work only; it does not reduce any Unpaid Balance or affect Sections 6 through 8.

## **3. Compensation — Collective Seat**

1.  **Admission.** The Members admitted the Contributor to the Contractor Pool by Supermajority vote on \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (date), per Section 3.4 of the Operating Agreement.
2.  **Threshold.** The Contributor’s Threshold is $\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ per ☐ distribution period ☐ fiscal year.
3.  **Earned Amount.** For each period during which this Agreement is in effect, the Contributor earns an amount equal to the Threshold for that period, prorated by the portion of the period during which this Agreement was in effect (the “Earned Amount”). Earned Amounts accrue from service rendered, whether or not the Company distributes anything for that period.
4.  **Unpaid Balance.** The Contributor’s “Unpaid Balance” at any time is the sum of all Earned Amounts less all amounts the Contributor has actually received under this Section. The Unpaid Balance carries forward from period to period and does not expire.
5.  **Mechanics.** Each time the Company makes a Seat distribution, the Contractor Pool receives one Seat’s share. That share is divided equally among everyone in the Pool who holds an Unpaid Balance, whether or not they are still performing services. No one receives more than their Unpaid Balance: any excess is re-divided equally among those whose Balances are not yet paid to zero, and anything left after every Balance is paid goes to the Member Seats.
6.  **A Threshold is a rate, not a cap.** The Threshold sets how fast the Contributor earns; the only limit on a single payment is the Unpaid Balance. A period in which the Company distributes little or nothing does not consume, forfeit, or shrink what the Contributor has earned. Work performed in a lean period is paid out of a later good one.
7.  **Contingency.** Payments under this Section come only from the Contractor Pool’s Seat share, and only when the Company has a distributable surplus. A period with no distribution pays nothing. An Unpaid Balance is a standing claim on future Collective Seat distributions and nothing more: it is not a debt of the Company or of any Member, not an accrued or guaranteed liability, and not a claim against anyone else in the Pool. The Contributor never bears Loss and never has to return compensation.
8.  **The Unpaid Balance survives the engagement.** Termination ends the Contributor’s obligation to perform Services and the accrual of new Earned Amounts, but does not reduce the Unpaid Balance. The Contributor keeps sharing in Collective Seat distributions under paragraph 5 until it is paid to zero — however long that takes, and whether or not the Company had any revenue, or the Contributor’s work had shipped, while this Agreement was in effect.
9.  **On admission as a Member.** If the Contributor is admitted as a Member under Section 9, any Unpaid Balance survives and continues to be paid under paragraph 5 until paid to zero. It is compensation for work performed before membership, and admission neither forfeits it nor converts it into an ownership interest.
10. **No membership.** Participation in the Pool is compensation for services only. It confers no membership, no governance vote, no ownership, and no interest in Company profits as such.
11. **Your Balance cannot be changed out from under you.** No amendment to the Operating Agreement or to the Contractor Pool’s rules reduces an Unpaid Balance already accrued or changes how it is paid; the terms in effect when an amount accrued continue to govern it. The Contributor may enforce Sections 3.3, 3.4, and 8.6 of the Operating Agreement directly against the Company.
12. **You can see the math.** On request at any time, the Company tells the Contributor how many people are then in the Pool, the total of all Unpaid Balances, and the calculation of any distribution affecting the Contributor.

**How the Collective Seat works in practice.** Say the Pool’s Seat share for a period is $12,000, and three Contributors hold Unpaid Balances of $8,000, $5,000, and $1,000.

  - Split equally three ways: $4,000 each.
  - C’s Balance is only $1,000, so C takes $1,000 and the extra $3,000 goes back in the pot.
  - Split that $3,000 between A and B: $1,500 each. B is now at $5,500 against a $5,000 Balance, so B takes $5,000 and $500 goes back in the pot.
  - A takes the last $500.

| Contributor | Balance before | Receives this period | Balance after |
| :---- | ----: | ----: | ----: |
| A | $8,000 | $6,000 | $2,000 |
| B | $5,000 | $5,000 | $0 |
| C | $1,000 | $1,000 | $0 |
| **Total** | **$14,000** | **$12,000** | **$2,000** |

A’s remaining $2,000 does not expire and is not reduced by anything. It is simply first in line next time.

**Transparency.** The Contributor acknowledges that, consistent with the Operating Agreement, the Contributor’s engagement form and Threshold are visible to all Members.

## **4. Independent Contractor Status**

1.  The Contributor is an independent contractor, not an employee, partner, member, or agent of the Company, and has no authority to bind the Company.
2.  The Contributor is responsible for their own taxes, including self-employment taxes. The Company will issue any required information returns (for example, Form 1099).
3.  The Contributor is not eligible for employee benefits and is not covered by the Company’s insurance except as required by law.
4.  The Contributor provides their own equipment and tools unless Exhibit 1 states otherwise.
5.  **If a government agency disagrees.** If any authority determines the Contributor was an employee rather than an independent contractor, this Agreement stays in effect except where the law requires otherwise; Sections 5 through 8 are unaffected; and amounts already paid under Section 3 are credited against any amount then found owing as wages. Neither party waives any position on the question by signing this.

## **5. Intellectual Property**

1.  **Work made for hire; assignment.** All work product the Contributor creates in performing the Services, including the Music defined in Section 6 (the “Work Product”), is a “work made for hire” for the Company to the fullest extent permitted by law. To the extent any Work Product is not a work made for hire, the Contributor hereby irrevocably assigns to the Company, upon creation, all right, title, and interest in it, including all copyrights, patent rights, trademark rights, and trade secrets, worldwide.
2.  **Moral rights.** To the extent permitted by law, the Contributor waives, and agrees not to assert, any moral rights in the Work Product against the Company or its licensees.
3.  **Further assurances.** The Contributor will execute any documents the Company reasonably requests to perfect or record the Company’s ownership.
4.  **Pre-existing materials.** Tools, libraries, and materials the Contributor created before or outside this engagement and listed in Exhibit 2 remain the Contributor’s. To the extent the Contributor incorporates any of them into Work Product, the Contributor grants the Company a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to use them as embedded in the Work Product and its derivatives.
5.  **What the Contributor warrants — and the limit on it.** The Contributor represents that the Work Product is their own original work, except for the samples, loops, and third-party, open-source, marketplace, and AI-generated components they identify — in Exhibit 2, or in writing to the Company on delivery — with the license terms each comes under; that it infringes no one’s rights; and that they have the right to grant what this Section grants. The Company relies on this to keep the Work Pool clean and to keep its promise that every Member may take the work with them. The Contributor’s total liability under this Agreement is limited to the amounts the Company has actually paid them, except for a breach of this paragraph or for willful misconduct. The Company’s liability to the Contributor is not limited.

**Why the Company takes ownership.** The Company cannot license or protect work it does not own, and it promises every Member the right to take its work with them (Section 7.3 of the Operating Agreement). So it owns everything the Contributor makes for it, music included. Section 6 is the safety net: composing for a game that is not yet funded is a risk, and if the funding never comes, the Contributor shares ownership of their music and can earn from it independently. The long-term path is membership, which brings the same perpetual license to all Company IP that every Member holds.

## **6. Music and the Kickstarter**

1.  **Terms.** “Music” means every musical composition and sound recording the Contributor creates in performing the Services, including stems, alternate mixes, and session files. A “Campaign” is a Kickstarter campaign the Company runs for the Game.
2.  **Kickstarter Success** occurs when a Campaign ends successfully funded under Kickstarter’s rules. The Company then remains the Music’s sole owner.
3.  **Kickstarter Failure** occurs on the earliest of these dates:
      - (a) thirty (30) days after a Campaign ends unfunded — at its deadline, or by cancellation or suspension — if no new Campaign has gone live by then;
      - (b) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (the “Outside Date”; if left blank, eighteen (18) months after the Start Date), if no Campaign has gone live by then; or
      - (c) the date the Company dissolves, or tells the Contributor in writing that it will run no further Campaign.
4.  **Only the last Campaign counts.** An unfunded Campaign followed within thirty (30) days by a new one is not a failure. Whichever of Kickstarter Success and Kickstarter Failure happens first is final; a later Campaign changes nothing. The Company will confirm the outcome to the Contributor in writing within ten (10) days, but the outcome does not depend on that confirmation.
5.  **On Kickstarter Failure, the Contributor becomes a co-owner.** The Company hereby assigns to the Contributor, effective on Kickstarter Failure, an undivided \_\_\_\_\_% share (if left blank, 50%) of the copyright in the Music — both the compositions and the recordings — and the same share of any Music created afterward, as it is created. The Members approved this assignment in the same vote that admitted the Contributor to the Contractor Pool (Section 3.1). On request, the Company will sign a short confirmation of the Contributor’s share for any distributor, label, or platform.
6.  **How co-ownership works.** Each co-owner — the Company and the Contributor — may do the following without the other’s consent:
      - (a) use, sell, distribute, stream, perform, and adapt the Music, anywhere;
      - (b) license the Music to anyone, non-exclusively; and
      - (c) transfer its own share, provided the person acquiring it agrees in writing to be bound by this Section.

    Each co-owner gives that consent now, irrevocably, and keeps everything it earns from the Music without owing the other any part of it. Neither may grant a license that is exclusive against the other; beyond that, the Contributor’s share does not limit or encumber the Company’s rights. The Contributor may describe the Music, truthfully, as written for the Game. Members keep the license to the Music that Section 7.3 of the Operating Agreement gives them in all Company IP.
7.  **No content claims.** Neither co-owner will use YouTube Content ID or any similar system against videos or streams that show the Game, whoever makes them, or against the other co-owner’s uses of the Music. Each will release any such claim, including one its distributor makes automatically, within five (5) business days of notice.
8.  **Pay is unaffected.** This Section does not change the Contributor’s compensation under Section 3, whichever way the Kickstarter goes.

## **7. Social Media and Portfolio — Always Yours**

1.  **The freedom.** The Contributor may create, publish, and share — anywhere, at any time, monetized or not, and without anyone’s approval — videos, streams, posts, devlogs, portfolio pieces, reels, and any other content that shows or uses the Game (including unreleased builds), the Music, the Game’s other assets, or any part of the process of making them (“Contributor Content”). The Company grants the Contributor a worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free license to do so, including to show the Game’s name and imagery, as far as the Company’s own rights allow. Everything Contributor Content earns belongs to the Contributor.
2.  **The only limits.** This Section overrides Section 10. Contributor Content may not disclose access credentials, other people’s personal data, or information the Company holds in confidence for a third party. Those are not the Company’s to give, and every Member works under the same limits (Section 7.6 of the Operating Agreement).
3.  **Selling the Music is separate.** Contributor Content does not include selling recordings of the Music, distributing it through music stores or music-streaming services, or licensing it to others. Section 6 governs those.
4.  **The Company will not interfere.** The Company will not claim, monetize, mute, block, or seek removal of Contributor Content, except content that breaks paragraph 2. It will release any such claim, including one its distributor makes automatically, within five (5) business days of notice.
5.  **Either way.** This Section applies whether the Kickstarter succeeds or fails.

## **8. Credit**

The Contributor will be credited by name (or a professional name they designate in writing) in the credits of any released project to which they contributed. Failure to credit due to good-faith error will be corrected in the next practicable update. The Company will require this credit obligation in any agreement under which a third party publishes or distributes a project the Contributor worked on; the Company does not trade away its workers’ names.

## **9. Path to Candidacy and Membership**

**Candidacy begins on your first day.** The Candidacy Period described in Section 2.3 of the Operating Agreement starts on the Start Date and runs six (6) months. The Contributor does not apply for it, no one approves it, and it cannot be withheld. Time already worked for the Company — paid or volunteer — counts toward the six months.

**The Company votes before the sixth month ends.** The Contributor receives the result in writing within thirty (30) days of the vote. If admission is declined, that writing states plainly where the Members see the misalignment — in the work, in the fit, or in the Company’s own capacity to carry another Member. It is a statement of reasons, not a promise that any particular change would produce a different answer.

**The engagement does not outlast the decision.** If the Contributor is not admitted by the end of the sixth month, this Agreement ends under Section 2.4, unless the Contributor has declined membership in writing. The Company does not keep people working as non-owners indefinitely.

**What membership is.** No buy-in, an equal seat, an equal share, and an equal vote — the same as every other Member, from the first day. On admission this Agreement terminates and the person’s work is governed by the Operating Agreement, including the perpetual license to Company IP and the right to be handed the actual files under Section 7.3 of the Operating Agreement. Any Unpaid Balance survives admission and keeps being paid (Section 3.9), and Sections 6 and 7 continue to apply.

## **10. Confidentiality — Narrow by Design**

1.  **What is confidential.** “Confidential Information” means only: unreleased builds, source code, and assets not yet made public; access credentials and keys; personal data of players, community members, Members, and other contributors; and third parties’ information the Company is obligated to protect.
2.  **What is never confidential.** Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Contributor from publishing Contributor Content under Section 7, or from discussing or disclosing their own compensation, their working conditions, the fact and general nature of this engagement, or from any communication protected by law, including Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act. Whether or not that Act reaches this engagement, the Company will not restrict these discussions as a matter of contract. The Company practices candid discourse and does not use confidentiality to silence workers.
3.  **Duration.** Confidentiality obligations end when the information becomes public through no breach, and in any event three (3) years after termination — except for personal data and third-party obligations, which last as long as the underlying duty.

## **11. No Exclusivity**

This engagement is non-exclusive in both directions. The Contributor may work for others, including other studios, and the Company may engage others for similar services. The Company encourages outside work and asks only that the Contributor avoid unauthorized use of Confidential Information or Work Product.

## **12. Dispute Resolution**

The parties will first attempt in good faith to resolve any dispute by direct, candid discussion, and may agree to mediation before pursuing other remedies. Nothing in this Agreement waives the Contributor’s non-waivable statutory rights, including the right to pursue harassment or discrimination claims before the appropriate agency.

2.  This Agreement, with its Exhibits, is the entire agreement about its subject and supersedes prior discussions. It may be amended only in a writing signed by both parties.
3.  If any provision is held unenforceable, the rest remains in effect.
4.  This Agreement may be signed in counterparts, including electronically.
5.  Where this Agreement conflicts with the Operating Agreement, the Operating Agreement controls as among the Company and its Members; this Agreement controls as to the Contributor’s compensation and rights stated here.
6. 7.  The Company may not assign this Agreement, including in a sale, merger, or reorganization of the Company, without the Contributor’s written consent. The Contributor may not assign it without the Company’s written consent, except that an Unpaid Balance passes to the Contributor’s estate.
